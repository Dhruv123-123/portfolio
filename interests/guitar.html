Guitar

Guitar · started in 2026

Background

This is the newest one. I listen to rock almost exclusively and have been drifting toward metal for the last year, and at some point in early 2026 it stopped being enough to just hear the riffs. I wanted to know what they felt like under my fingers. So I bought a used Squier Stratocaster off Craigslist from a guy in the Mission who was upgrading, along with a small practice amp that I've been told sounds bad and that I can't hear the problem with yet.

The first weeks

Nobody warns you about the calluses. For about two weeks my fingertips hurt constantly and I couldn't type properly, which is a real problem when typing is your job. Then one morning they didn't hurt anymore and the strings stopped buzzing, and I understood why every guitarist talks about this stage like a rite of passage.

I'm learning the way everyone learns now: a structured YouTube course for the fundamentals, tabs for the songs, and a metronome app that I resent. The F chord took eleven days. I counted.

What I'm working on

The plan is to learn the songs that made me want to play in the first place, in roughly the order I can physically manage them:

  • Smells Like Teen Spirit, which was the first thing I could play all the way through and which I have now played far too many times.
  • Seven Nation Army, because it's the law.
  • Enter Sandman, which is where I am now: the main riff is fine and the rest is aspirational.
  • Master of Puppets, at 60% speed, with the down-picking that makes my forearm feel like it's being slowly filled with sand. This is the goal. I'm a long way off.

Why it's good to be bad at something

Most of what I do, I've been doing for years. Cubing, racing, Pokémon: I'm at least competent at all of them, and competence is comfortable. The guitar is the first thing in a long time where I'm properly, obviously bad, where a twelve-year-old on YouTube is doing effortlessly what I can't do at all, and it turns out that's its own kind of fun. Every week something that was impossible becomes merely hard. I'd forgotten what that felt like.

Conclusion

I practice most days, usually late, usually with headphones so the neighbors don't have to hear the F chord. It's the thing I'm currently trying hardest to get genuinely good at. Ask me in a year.
