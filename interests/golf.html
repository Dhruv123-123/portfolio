Golf

Golf · since 2021

Background

My dad started taking me to a driving range in Bengaluru when I was fifteen, mostly as an excuse to talk, and for the first several months I could not hit the ball. Not badly. At all. Then one evening something clicked and a seven iron went exactly where I was looking, high and straight and quiet, and I understood immediately that I was going to spend the rest of my life chasing that feeling. That's the whole game. Everyone who plays is chasing one shot they hit once.

Where I play

The Bay Area is a good place to be a bad golfer with no money. Lincoln Park in San Francisco is a scruffy little city course where the seventeenth tee has a view of the Golden Gate Bridge that people pay hundreds of dollars for elsewhere. Tilden Park in the Berkeley hills has deer on the fairways and a twilight rate that makes a round cost less than dinner. I play early mornings or late evenings, whenever the tee sheet is empty and nobody's behind me.

The state of my game

Bogey golf on a good day. My driver is a liability I keep in the bag out of optimism. My short game is the only part that's actually decent, which I attribute to spending far more time chipping onto a rug in my apartment than any adult should. I have one birdie on a par 3 that I've described to more people than have asked, and a scorecard from a round where I shot 89 that I've kept for two years.

Why

It's the slow one. Everything else I do is fast: fast cars, fast cubes, a job where things change weekly. Golf is four hours where the only problem in the world is the next shot, the phone stays in the bag, and the person you're walking with has nowhere else to be either. I'll take that trade every time.

Conclusion

I'd like to break 85 this year, which requires my driver and me to reach some kind of understanding. I'd also like to get my dad out to Lincoln Park for the seventeenth hole. He's the reason I play and he's never seen it.
