Golf

Golf · since 2021

My dad started taking me to a driving range when I was about fifteen, mostly as an excuse to hang out, and for the first few months I genuinely could not hit the ball. Then one evening a seven iron just went, high and straight and dead quiet, and I knew right then I was going to be chasing that feeling for the rest of my life. That's basically the whole game. Everyone who plays is trying to hit one shot they hit once.

The Bay Area is a good place to be a bad golfer without a lot of money. Lincoln Park in SF is a scrappy little city course where the 17th tee has a view of the Golden Gate that people pay hundreds of dollars for elsewhere. Tilden in the Berkeley hills has deer on the fairways and a twilight rate that's cheaper than dinner. I mostly go early mornings or late evenings when nobody's behind me.

Bogey golf on a good day. My driver is a liability I keep in the bag out of optimism, as the video up top makes pretty clear. My short game is the one part that's actually decent, probably because I chip onto a rug in my apartment more than any adult should. I have one birdie on a par 3 that I've told more people about than have asked, and I kept the scorecard from the round where I shot 89.

Everything else I do is fast. Golf is four hours where the only problem is the next shot and the phone stays in the bag. I want to break 85 this year, which means my driver and I need to work some things out. I'd also like to get my dad out to Lincoln Park for the 17th hole. He's the reason I play and he's never seen it.
