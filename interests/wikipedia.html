Wikipedia

Reading · a lifelong problem

Background

It started with a school project on the Ottoman Empire that was supposed to take an evening. By midnight I had forty tabs open and was reading about the Great Fire of Smyrna, which was not in the assignment, and I have never really closed those tabs since. I can go for hours on Wikipedia and read absolutely everything. It's the closest thing I have to a vice.

How a session goes

A typical one, reconstructed from browser history: I look up the Habsburg jaw because it came up in conversation. That leads to Charles II of Spain, who leads to the War of the Spanish Succession, which leads to the Treaty of Utrecht, which is how Britain got Gibraltar, which is how there are monkeys in Gibraltar, which leads to the Barbary macaque, which leads to the legend that Britain will lose Gibraltar if the monkeys leave, which is how Churchill personally ordered more monkeys in 1942. It's 2am. I know far too much about the Habsburg succession and I have also learned something important about monkeys.

Every article has a blue link in the second paragraph that is more interesting than the article, and I have never once been able to resist it.

Favorite corners

The good stuff is in the lists and the long tail:

  • The list of common misconceptions, which I reread once a year and which has ruined several dinner parties.
  • The list of unusual deaths, which goes back to antiquity and gets funnier the further back you go.
  • Every aviation accident article ever written, which is where a different interest of mine started.
  • Talk pages on contested articles, where you can watch two strangers argue about a comma for eleven years.
  • Lists of lists of lists, which exists, and which is the closest Wikipedia comes to a joke about itself.

Wiki Race

The upside of all this is that I am genuinely, unreasonably good at Wiki Race: two random articles, and you have to get from one to the other using only the links on the page. Most people click on whatever looks relevant. The trick is to think in hubs. Almost everything links to a country, every country links to a date, every date links to a person, and from a big enough article, like the United States or the Second World War or Philosophy, you can get anywhere in the encyclopedia in four moves.

I can beat anyone. This is a standing offer. Pick any two articles.

Conclusion

I've tried to justify this as research or general knowledge, and sometimes it is. Mostly it's just that the world is enormous and someone has written it all down and put links between the pieces, and I find it impossible to stop pulling on the thread.
