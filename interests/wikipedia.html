Wikipedia

This started with a school project that was supposed to take one evening. I had like forty tabs open by midnight and was reading about stuff that had nothing to do with the assignment, and honestly I've never really closed those tabs. I can go for hours on Wikipedia just reading absolutely everything, going from one article to the next through the links.

I'll look something up because it came up in conversation, say the Habsburg jaw. That takes me to Charles II of Spain, then the War of the Spanish Succession, then the Treaty of Utrecht, which is how Britain got Gibraltar, which is why there are monkeys in Gibraltar, which gets me to the legend that Britain loses Gibraltar if the monkeys leave, which is how I find out Churchill ordered more monkeys in 1942. And now it's 2am.

There's always a link in the second paragraph that's more interesting than the article you came for, and I've never once been able to leave it alone.

Some of the stuff I keep going back to:

  • The list of common misconceptions, which I reread like once a year.
  • The list of unusual deaths, which gets funnier the further back in history you go.
  • Basically every aviation accident article, which is where a whole other interest of mine came from.
  • Talk pages on controversial articles, where you can watch two people argue about a comma for a decade.
  • Lists of lists of lists, which is a real page.

The upside of all this is that I'm really good at Wiki Race, where you get two random articles and have to get from one to the other using only the links on the page. The trick is to think in hubs. Almost everything links to a country, every country links to a date, every date links to a person, and from a big enough article like the United States or World War II you can get anywhere in like four clicks. I can beat anyone at it. Genuinely, pick two articles.
