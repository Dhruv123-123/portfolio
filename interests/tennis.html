Tennis

Tennis · 2014–2022

Background

Tennis was the first thing I was ever serious about. I started at eight, at a club in Bengaluru with cracked hard courts and a coach who believed in running as a moral virtue, and for most of my childhood I was on court five days a week before school. I played junior tournaments across Karnataka and was, for a while, genuinely decent.

The forehand and the other thing

I had one weapon, a heavy topspin forehand that I hit off both wings if I could get away with it, and I built my entire game around never having to hit a backhand. My coach spent eight years trying to fix this. He did not succeed. I would run around the ball to the point of absurdity, and when opponents figured it out, which good ones did quickly, my tournaments ended.

Why I stopped

Two things: the cube and the pandemic. Cubing took over my free time around fourteen, and then 2020 closed every court in the city for the better part of a year. By the time they reopened I'd found running, which needed no court, no partner, and no backhand. I played my last tournament at sixteen and didn't miss it as much as I expected to.

Conclusion

I still hit occasionally, and the forehand still works. Tennis gave me the thing every sport since has relied on: the ability to lose on a Saturday morning in front of my parents and show up again Sunday. Everything else is details.
