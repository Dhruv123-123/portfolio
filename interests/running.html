Running

Running · XC and track, then a comeback

\n

Background

\n

I got into running the honest way: someone made me. I joined cross country in high school mostly because my friends did, and discovered that I was decent at suffering in a straight line for long periods, which is most of what the sport is. I ran track too, where the suffering is shorter and much more public. Those seasons taught me the thing I still believe about running, that it's one of the only sports where you get out almost exactly what you put in, with no talent tax and nowhere to hide.

\n
\n
\n

The washed era

\n

Then I stopped. Not on purpose, the way nobody ever stops on purpose: a startup, a move, a few months of "I'll go tomorrow," and suddenly I am, by any fair reading of my most recent Strava activity, insanely washed. Exhibit A: a Sunday morning out-and-back along Ocean Beach on the Great Highway, 6.25 miles at 9:11 pace, after which Strava congratulated me on my fastest 10K of the year. That is not a compliment. My high school self would be appalled, and honestly he'd be right.

\n
\n
\n

Back on the grind

\n

So I'm getting back on it. The plan is the same one that worked when I was sixteen, which is to run more than feels reasonable and complain about it to anyone who'll listen. San Francisco helps: the Embarcadero is flat and long, the Marina is where you go to be seen running, and the Berkeley hills exist for the days I want to remember what XC felt like.

\n

There are two things on the calendar to keep me honest: the Diplo 5K in San Francisco, which I intend to run fast and in questionable clothing, and the Berkeley Half, which I intend to finish without stopping to reconsider my choices somewhere around mile nine.

\n
\n
\n

Conclusion

\n

It's the cheap one: shoes, a door, and however far I feel like going that day. It's also where most of my thinking gets done, and I've missed that more than the fitness. Check back after the half.
