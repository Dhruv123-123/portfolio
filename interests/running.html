Running

Running · daily-ish

Background

I started running in the spring of 2020 for the same reason half the planet did: everything was closed and I needed to leave the house. Cubbon Park in Bengaluru at six in the morning, before the heat, three slow loops of the same path. I was terrible and it was miserable and I kept doing it, and somewhere around the second month it stopped being miserable and became the part of the day I looked forward to.

Routes

The Bay Area is almost unfair for running. From my apartment I can be on the Embarcadero in minutes and run flat along the water all the way to Crissy Field with the bridge getting bigger the whole time. When I want to suffer, the Berkeley fire trails above campus go straight up into the hills and reward you with the entire bay laid out below. Strawberry Canyon has broken me more than once and I keep going back.

Numbers

I'm not fast. My 5k best is a little under 22 minutes, set on a good day with a tailwind and something to prove. I've run one half marathon, in 2025, and I finished it, and I said never again at the finish line, and I have already signed up for another one. Mostly I run four or five times a week, forty minutes, no watch, no music, at whatever pace my legs decide.

Why

It's the cheap one: shoes, a door, and however far I feel like going that day. But the real reason is that it's where most of my thinking gets done. I've solved more bugs on the Embarcadero than at my desk. Something about the rhythm and the lack of a screen lets the problem rearrange itself, and I get back and the answer is just there. I don't fully understand it. I've stopped trying to.

Conclusion

The half marathon is in the fall. The goal is to finish without saying never again. I don't rate my chances.
