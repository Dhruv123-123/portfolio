Running

Running · XC and track, now a comeback

Background

I got into running through cross country in high school, mostly because my friends did it, and it turned out I was decent at suffering in a straight line for a long time, which is most of what the sport is. I ran track too, where the suffering is shorter but a lot more public. It's one of the only sports where you get out almost exactly what you put in, there's no talent tax and nowhere to hide, and I think that's why it stuck with me.

Getting washed

Then I stopped, the way nobody really means to stop. A startup, a move, a bunch of "I'll go tomorrow," and now, as you can see from my most recent Strava activity, I'm insanely washed. That was a Sunday morning out and back along Ocean Beach, 6.25 miles at 9:11 pace, and Strava congratulated me on my fastest 10K of the year, which tells you everything. Paces that used to be a warmup are now a workout. High school me would be appalled.

Back on the grind

So I'm getting back on the grind. The plan is the same one that worked when I was sixteen, run more than feels reasonable and complain about it to whoever will listen. SF helps, the Embarcadero is flat and long and the Berkeley hills are there for the days I want to remember what XC felt like.

I'm signed up for the Diplo 5K in SF and the Berkeley Half to keep me honest. The 5K I want to run fast. The half I mostly want to finish without stopping to rethink my life around mile nine.

Why

It's the cheapest hobby I have, shoes and a door and that's it. It's also where I do most of my thinking, and I've missed that more than the fitness. Check back after the half.
