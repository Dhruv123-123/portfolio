Pokémon

Pokémon · since 2013

Background

When I was six my cousin gave me his old Game Boy Advance SP and a single cartridge: Pokémon Sapphire, with a save file he'd already beaten. I deleted it, which he still brings up, and started over. The first choice the game asks you to make is which of three creatures you want as your partner, and I picked the small blue one with the fin because it looked like it needed a friend. That was Mudkip. Roughly a hundred and fifty hours later it was a Swampert, and I had opinions.

I have played every single mainline Pokémon game released since. Not most of them. Every one.

Why Sapphire

Sapphire is the best game in the series and I'm not taking questions, but I'll take the opportunity to explain. Hoenn is the only region that feels like a real place with real weather. Half the map is water and you dive under it. There's a volcano that rains ash, a desert with a sandstorm you have to earn goggles to enter, and a soundtrack built almost entirely out of trumpets. The villains want to flood the world for the benefit of water Pokémon, which as a six-year-old with a Mudkip I found completely reasonable.

And Swampert. A Water and Ground type, which means the single most common attack type in the game does nothing to it. Enormous physical bulk. A face like it has just been told something disappointing. It's the best Pokémon and it isn't close.

Every single game

Some opinions, formed over roughly two decades and a frankly concerning number of hours:

  • Platinum is the best-designed game. Sapphire is still the best game. These are different things.
  • Black and White had the best story and the worst reception, and the internet was wrong.
  • Legends: Arceus was the most fun I've had with the series since I was a kid, and I'd like a second one.
  • Scarlet and Violet ran badly, looked worse, and I still put three hundred hours into them.
  • Nobody has ever made a good Pokémon game about ice types. I think about this more than I should.

Competitive

The story games are one thing. Competitive Pokémon, the VGC doubles format, is something else entirely: a two-versus-two card game played with animals, where team building matters as much as the battles, and where a single unexpected item on an opponent's Pokémon can undo three weeks of planning. I started playing it seriously in 2022 and in February 2023 I finished 6th in VGC Seniors at the Fresno Regional.

Chi-Yu and Flutter Mane did most of the damage. Amoonguss and Iron Hands kept them alive long enough to do it. Gyarados was there to Intimidate people and Thunder Wave anything that got ideas, and Baxcalibur cleaned up. Not a single water starter in sight. I'm as surprised as you are.

Conclusion

There is a Swampert figure on my desk next to the Rubik's cube. Every new game gets played on release week. When the next Hoenn remake comes out, and it will, I'll be there at midnight, and I'll pick Mudkip again.
