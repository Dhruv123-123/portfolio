Pokémon

Pokémon · since 2013

Background

Pokémon is by far my favorite video game and media franchise of all time. It started in 2013, when a family friend gave my brother and me a black Game Boy Advance SP with two cartridges: Pokémon Sapphire and Pokémon FireRed. We played those games to death. On our very first playthrough we picked Mudkip, evolved it all the way into a Swampert, and then kept going until it was level 83, which is a completely unnecessary level for anything in that game and exactly the kind of thing two kids with one Game Boy do. It still brings back good memories.

Every generation since

I've played every single generation of Pokémon game since. Sapphire is still my favorite, and Pearl is a close second: Hoenn for the water and the weather and the trumpets, Sinnoh for the atmosphere and the best rival in the series. Swampert is my favorite Pokémon and, given how it started, it was never going to be anything else.

I still have the collection, and I'm still growing it. Every new game gets played on release week, and the old cartridges get pulled out more often than I'd admit to.

Competitive

The story games are one thing. Competitive Pokémon, the VGC doubles format, is something else: team building matters as much as the battles, and a single unexpected item on an opponent's Pokémon can undo weeks of planning. I've played it seriously for a few years, been ranked as high as 400th on the global leaderboard, and top cut a regional tournament, finishing 6th in VGC Seniors at the 2023 Fresno Regional. The team is below.

Chi-Yu and Flutter Mane did most of the damage. Amoonguss and Iron Hands kept them alive long enough to do it. Gyarados was there to Intimidate people and Thunder Wave anything that got ideas, and Baxcalibur cleaned up.

Conclusion

There is a Swampert figure on my desk next to the Rubik's cube. When the next Hoenn remake comes out, and it will, I'll be there at midnight, and I'll pick Mudkip again.
