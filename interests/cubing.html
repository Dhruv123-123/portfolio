Rubik's cube

Cubing · since 2016

Background

I got a Rubik's cube when I was around ten and learned it off a YouTube tutorial. My first solve took a little over four minutes and I made everyone in the house watch. That was the beginner method, and for about a year I thought that was all there was to it. Then I found out the actual speedcubers use a method called CFOP and that the world record was under five seconds, and that kind of broke my brain a little.

Learning the algorithms

CFOP ends with two stages, OLL and PLL, and between them there are 78 cases you have to learn. Most people learn a few and work around the rest. I decided to learn all of them, mostly because I was thirteen and had a lot of free time. I made flashcards and drilled them on the school bus until they were just in my hands.

These days I average sub-18 on 3x3 and sub-3 on 2x2. I can also solve it blindfolded, which is a totally different thing where you memorize the whole cube as a string of letters and then execute it with your eyes closed. I'm not fast at blind, but it works, and it's a good party trick.

The world record

In January 2020 I was one of seven kids who solved 736 cubes in one hour at Mantri Square Mall in Bengaluru, which got us a world record for the most cubes solved by a team of kids in an hour. The hard part was actually the logistics, keeping the scramblers ahead of the solvers and making sure two people didn't grab the same cube. My hands were cramping by minute forty and I honestly don't remember much of the last fifteen minutes.

The videos

There are videos of all of this, including me at competitions with braces and a haircut I didn't pick. They're too embarrassing to put up here.

Now

I keep a cube on my desk at work and solve it while stuff is running. I'd like to get sub-15 eventually but that means learning a bunch of new algorithms and I'm not thirteen anymore.
